Claude · motivation v2

Three dashboards, rebuilt after reading the other agent's four.

v1 had five directions and a lot of data. Codex had four with sharper semantics: opportunities instead of calendar days, a "try today" control, partial routines, activities separate from projects, and deliverables as tasks. This version keeps what survived that comparison and drops what didn't. It stands alone — no links back to v1.

Build first: Finish Line, on one goal with complete data — meditation minutes. Pull-ups follow the day the exercise spreadsheet import lands. Reasoning is at the bottom.

The three pitches

01 · FINISH LINE

What tonight does to the rest of the year

"27 reps gets you to 5,000 — and drops tomorrow's number from 47.3 to 46.9 per training day."

Hero
Your spreadsheet's four numbers (done, your rate, needed from tomorrow, vs. even pace) for six finish-line goals, and a slider that moves the chart, not just the text. Drag it and the needed ray swings, the next-threshold line gets crossed, and a gold "that stays done" appears.
Key change
Everything counts eligible opportunities. Pull-ups count Mon/Tue/Thu/Sat, so "needed" is per training day. The calendar version reads 27.0 — well under your 44.8, so it says you're comfortable when you're 2 training days behind.
Honesty
Workout sessions start Jan 1 but the app has nothing before Mar 2 — the spreadsheet isn't ported. The chart hatches that stretch and labels the total a floor. The pull-up goal shows the other answer: it starts Mar 2, where capture is complete.
Kept from v1
Required pace, day by day — the chart of what "needed" was on each past day — and gear (needed ÷ rate) as the sort key across units.

02 · TONIGHT

Thursday, 9:52pm: what fits, what finishes, what can wait

"Skincare completes tonight." · "One of the 2 drawing days still owed." · "Software — week already met."

Hero
A time budget, not a ranking. Pick 10 / 20 / 45 / any, and at most four invitations that fit appear, closest threshold first. The default is 20 because your own hour-of-day data says that's what you do after 10pm.
Key change
A section called Can honestly wait. When a week's intention is met the page says so and stops asking. Deep work is pinned to tomorrow morning instead of losing to ten-minute cards every night.
Details that matter
Winddown is a three-step routine with a 4am night boundary; partial nights stay partial. Tuesday's 10-minute drawing session shows as logged but not qualifying. The book card says "84 pages to the end" and is labelled as needing proposed fields, while its "book 17 of 24" line works today from reading-log/finished?.

Where the creative hours actually go

Hero

Schema, re-ranked

1 · goal

[:goal/label        :string]
[:goal/source-type  [:enum :exercise-line :exercise-session :meditation-log
                     :reading-log :project-log :habit-log]]
[:goal/metric       [:enum :reps :minutes :occurrences :active-days
                     :complete-routines :books-finished]]
;; typed, optional scopes — validated against the metric
[:goal/exercise-ids {:optional true} [:set :exercise/id]]
[:goal/activity-id  {:optional true} :activity/id]
[:goal/routine-id   {:optional true} :routine/id]
[:goal/target       :number]
[:goal/period       [:enum :fixed :week]]     ; fixed = start/end window
[:goal/starts-on    :local-date]
[:goal/ends-on      {:optional true} :local-date]
[:goal/weekdays     {:optional true} [:set [:enum :mon :tue :wed :thu :fri :sat :sun]]]
[:goal/min-per-day  {:optional true} :number]    ; what makes a day "qualify"
[:goal/time-zone    :string]

One point neither v1 nor Codex made: goal revisions are already free. Codex rightly says "preserve revisions; don't rewrite old expectations silently" — and XTDB keeps every version of a document. When you lower a target in October, entity-history (inside db/queries.clj, per the project rules) can still draw the old line. No goal/supersedes-id needed.

2 · activity + project-log/activity-id + project/activity-id

The session says what you did; the project says what it served. The project's activity is only a default copied at capture. One conservative backfill at launch — single-activity projects only — then never again. This is the change that makes writing, drawing and music first-class without a new log type each.

3 · routine

[:routine/label :string] [:routine/habit-ids [:set :habit/id]]
[:routine/day-start-hour :int] [:routine/time-zone :string]

Same bitemporal note: evaluate each night against the routine as of that night, and adding skincare in October doesn't make every September night incomplete.

4 · book/page-count + reading-log/page-position

I'm conceding Codex's version over mine. Store where you stopped, not pages read, and never derive pages read from adjacent logs — skipped logs and rereads make that wrong. Page-count on the book is what unlocks "84 pages to the end", the only naturally bounded target in your data.

5 · exercise/movement-pattern + exercise/primary-region

Still my best cost-to-payoff pick and still missing from Codex. It goes on the entity, so a few dozen rows classify every set ever logged. Unlocks 046's muscle map and "you haven't trained a hinge in 19 days".

6 · task/milestone

One optional boolean. task/project-id, task/state and task/done-at already do the rest.

Not now

  • project/category — retracted; activity on the session does it properly.
  • project-log/output-count — later, and only for an explicit goal like "10,000 new words".
  • Satisfaction ratings, stored streaks or totals, per-practice log entities — still no, for v1's reasons.
  • Import coverage needs no schema: a per-source "complete since" date can live in code until there's a second source that needs it.

Rules the engine has to keep

  • Count opportunities, not calendar days, wherever the goal names weekdays.
  • A day qualifies once, no matter how many sessions; below the floor it's logged but doesn't count.
  • Crossed stays crossed. Past the target, needed is 0 and the excess is shown, not hidden.
  • Partial stays partial. Unclassified stays unclassified. Missing imports are marked, never estimated.
  • No life score. Nothing adds reps to minutes.
  • Reads follow db/queries.clj scan-then-pull: index-only tuple scan bounded by the goal window, daily fold in Clojure. Parent-scoped reads (one goal's exercise ids) are equality-bound.

Charting

ECharts for the burn-up (with live slider updates via setOption merges by series id), the required-pace history, the stacked attention bars, the milestone timeline and the hour-of-day band. Hand-rolled SVG for the 12-week grid, because rows must share one axis and cells need three visual states ECharts' calendar can't express. HTML for routine steps, day cells and big numbers — they're controls and text, not charts.

What I'd build first

Finish Line, one goal, meditation minutes.

  1. Goal entity + the series engine (window, weekdays, min-per-day, opportunities). Meditation needs nothing else: the log exists and the history is complete, so the loop can be judged on true numbers.
  2. Finish Line for that one goal, slider included. If a week of it doesn't pull you the way the spreadsheet did, stop and rethink before building anything else.
  3. Pull-ups the day the exercise import lands — it's the goal closest to your original, and the reason the start-date and coverage rules exist.
  4. Activity on project-log, then Studio's weekly intentions. That's when writing, drawing and music join the system.
  5. Tonight last: it's the page you'd open most, but it ranks and filters using every other piece, so it's only as good as the goals under it.
